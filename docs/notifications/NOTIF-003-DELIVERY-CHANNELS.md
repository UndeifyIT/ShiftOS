# 1. Purpose

This document defines the notification delivery channels supported by ShiftOS.

Delivery Channels determine how notifications are delivered after they have been generated by the Notification Service.

The delivery method depends on the notification type, priority, user preferences and organization policies.

---

# 2. Delivery Philosophy

The right information should reach the right user through the most appropriate channel.

Delivery should be:

- Reliable.
- Timely.
- Non-duplicative.
- Respectful of user preferences.
- Appropriate for the notification's priority.

---

# 3. Supported Delivery Channels

ShiftOS supports the following delivery channels.

### In-App Notifications

Displayed within the ShiftOS application.

Examples:

- New task assigned.
- Shift updated.
- Announcement published.

This is the primary delivery channel.

---

### Push Notifications

Delivered to supported mobile devices.

Examples:

- Upcoming shift reminder.
- Task due soon.
- Schedule published.

Push notifications should be concise and encourage users to open the application.

---

### Email Notifications

Delivered to the user's verified email address.

Examples:

- Organization invitation.
- Password reset.
- Security notifications.
- Important operational summaries.

Email should be used for information that remains valuable outside the application.

---

### Future Channels

Future versions may support:

- SMS.
- WhatsApp.
- Microsoft Teams.
- Slack.
- Webhooks.
- Third-party integrations.

---

# 4. Channel Selection

The Notification Service determines the appropriate delivery channel based on:

- Notification type.
- Priority level.
- User preferences.
- Organization policy.
- Device availability.
- Channel availability.

A notification may be delivered through one or more channels.

---

# 5. Multi-Channel Delivery

Some notifications require delivery through multiple channels.

Example:

```
Critical Security Event

↓

In-App Notification

+

Push Notification

+

Email Notification
```

Multi-channel delivery should be reserved for high-priority events.

---

# 6. Delivery Rules

Notifications should:

- Avoid unnecessary duplication.
- Respect quiet hours where applicable.
- Respect user notification preferences.
- Respect mandatory organization policies.

Critical operational notifications may override certain user preferences where permitted by organization policy.

---

# 7. Delivery Status

Each notification delivery attempt has one status.

Supported statuses include:

- Pending.
- Queued.
- Sent.
- Delivered.
- Failed.
- Expired.

Status tracking supports monitoring and retry processing.

---

# 8. Offline Delivery

If a user is offline:

- In-app notifications remain pending until the next session.
- Push notifications are handled by the platform notification service where supported.
- Email delivery proceeds independently.

Notifications should never be lost because a user is temporarily offline.

---

# 9. Permissions

Notification delivery never bypasses authorization.

Only users authorized to receive the underlying notification may receive it through any delivery channel.

Delivery channels do not alter notification visibility.

---

# 11. Audit Requirements

The following events may generate audit records:

- Notification queued.
- Notification sent.
- Notification delivered.
- Notification delivery failed.
- Notification delivery cancelled.

Operational events remain audited within their originating domains.
